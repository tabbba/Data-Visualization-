Introduction & Research Question

The Question We Set Out to Answer

The COVID-19 pandemic caused the sharpest collapse in aviation history. As the world reopened, European skies filled again: but did the recovery happen in a way that is compatible with climate commitments?

Our central finding is striking: 2024 flights are just +1.9% above 2019, yet CO₂ emissions are +14.1% above 2019, and CO₂ per flight is +11.9% higher. Traffic barely grew. Emissions surged.

Data Limitations

  • Emission figures are model-based EUROCONTROL estimates, not direct aircraft measurements.
  • Airport data covers IFR flights only.
  • Carbon price reflects market prices, not necessarily the effective cost paid by each airline.
  • Passenger load factor is unavailable at this granularity.

07: Visualisation Ethics

Same Countries, Different Recovery Story

The final section turns the analysis back on the visualisation process itself. Two static plots use the same country-level recovery data, but one compares traffic and CO₂ fairly while the other narrows the story to flight growth alone.

Critical Insight

Visualisation choices are analytical choices. Metric selection, ranking, scale, and visual emphasis can make the same dataset appear balanced, incomplete, or deceptive.

White Hat: Recovery Fairness

This chart compares each country's 2024 flight recovery with its 2024 CO₂ recovery, both indexed to 2019. Countries above the diagonal emitted more CO₂ than their traffic recovery alone would imply, while bubble size keeps the absolute 2024 CO₂ burden visible.

Black Hat: Selective Growth Ranking

This chart ranks countries only by percent flight growth above 2019. The values are real, but the framing hides whether CO₂ grew faster than traffic and ignores absolute emissions, making small fast-growing markets appear more central than they are climatically.

Why this matters

A chart can be technically based on real data and still be misleading. In this case, the black-hat version does not change the dataset; it changes the reader's perception by selecting a convenient metric and suppressing the emissions context needed to evaluate sustainability.
